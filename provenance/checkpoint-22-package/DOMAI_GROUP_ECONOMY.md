# DOMAI GROUP ECONOMY — group-split clarification and author elasticity

Canonical term remains lowercase `domai`.

## Group contribution award

Valnak evaluates the validated contribution of the participating group/formation and assigns a contextual **group contribution award**.

The base group award is then divided across the eligible members of that group/formation.

For an equal base split:

`base_member_share = group_award / eligible_members`

Examples are conceptual only:
- a military formation can generate a huge aggregate award but divide it across a very large roster, producing a modest per-person share;
- a Kira+illi Duo can generate a much smaller aggregate award, but each receives 50% of the Duo's base award.

Valnak does NOT pay more merely because a group is small.
The difference comes from how many people divide the group's earned award.

## Preserve participant eligibility / death rules

Preserve Checkpoint 18:
- first validated eldris kill starts the participant's 7-day eligibility window;
- conquest/core break inside the window can pay after departure;
- voluntary exit/death imposes one full day of same-domai lockout;
- individual death penalty remains cumulative ×0.8^deaths.

Apply the death multiplier to that participant's eventual share.
Exact rounding and interaction with any future individual bonus layer remain OPEN.

## Individual bonuses

A separate bonus for unusual individual contribution, core/final action, etc. is a possible direction but is **NOT locked** here.
Do not invent one unless authored later.

## Elastic Economic Actuator — AUTHOR ONLY

Author shorthand:
**`domai` = Elastic Economic Actuator.**

This is NOT an in-world term.

Because the aggregate contribution award is contextual and intentionally lacks a fixed rank formula, a `domai` can supply discretionary liquidity when the story benefits from it without changing the fixed Trial/Dungeon/Raid economy.

Author rule:
1. the `domai` occurs legitimately;
2. the contribution award is established/received;
3. only afterward does prose expose the social/personal purchase the credits make possible.

Never write the payout as an obvious earmarked quest reward.
