## Operations and conservation

Genesis energy expands accessible configuration space; conscious selection and collapse reassign inheritance from environment to ring. Recursive topology conserves identity, structure, topology and relationships. The object never ceases to exist; the ring does not contain a physical storage volume. Retrieval reverses inheritance into the environment through conscious recognition, selection and collapse, rather than conventional object transport.

More energy extends accessible configuration radius. Ring quality sets its maximum projection. Rings are civilization-standard because they are worn, durable, lightweight, hands-free and expedition-friendly; cubes, necklaces, bracelets and talismans may also exist.

## User experience and expedition role

Retrieval supplies no object list, menu, operating system or database. Novices struggle with unfamiliar topological identities; experienced users recognize items nearly instantly. User organization governs retrieval. The source’s Jenkins example—retrieving assorted junk before finding rope at a 300-foot chasm—illustrates organizational failure while the ring works correctly; it does not place Jenkins in MK157’s current calendar.

Professional delvers are expected to possess rings; better ones are major investments. Rings remove inventory bookkeeping while preserving decisions about routes, supplies and obstacles. A common delver says storage ring; a scholar describes configuration-space storage; an Architect understands recursive conservation and inheritance reassignment.
