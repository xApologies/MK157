# BUILDER COMMUNITY — community/UI layer over existing Builder registries

## Existing backend

Preserve `builder/paths/` and its 200 current candidate Legacy trajectories.
The Builder community is a presentation/social layer over existing Binding, summon and Legacy registries; do not regenerate the registries.

## Node Builder application

The Node exposes a dynamic Builder interface for planning and theorycrafting.

Core interactions:
- browse/search builds by relevant role/Legacy families and tags;
- inspect a build's Binding architecture, ranks/targets, optional components and projected purchase path;
- create a hypothetical build;
- save multiple builds to a participant profile;
- publish/share builds;
- lightweight positive/negative reactions (like/dislike);
- dynamic discovery surfaces such as Featured, Most Liked/Highest Rated, New, Trending and Valnak Recommended.

Do NOT turn this into a full forum/social network.
No mandatory strategy-guide essays, comments or external-web mechanics are required.

`Builder` remains the current author/system label; a final prose-facing in-world product name may remain OPEN.

## Elara comedy/culture

Elara publishes her own build ideas into the Builder community and cares about how they perform socially:
featured placement, likes/rating, competing popular variants, etc.
This is a comedy/social beat, not a Custodian privilege that makes her builds automatically correct.

## Theorycrafting culture

Builder use is intrinsically fun, not merely utilitarian.
People create:
- builds they intend to purchase;
- hypothetical builds they may never use;
- first-cycle budget variants;
- specialized Dungeon/Raid compositions;
- veteran retrospective builds ("what we used then / how I'd optimize it now");
- refinements of other public ideas.

First-cyclers are particularly active because every early purchase has opportunity cost, but veterans remain major participants because they enjoy build design and knowledge transfer.

## Builder parties

Builder parties are ordinary friend gatherings, not a formal institution:
food, cards, build screens, arguments, jokes, planning and sometimes later Training Yard testing.

They are one mechanism by which first-cycle cliques/friend groups form.
Dungeon and Raid acquaintances become repeat partners; repeat partners become Builder-party invitees; social relationships feed future groups.
